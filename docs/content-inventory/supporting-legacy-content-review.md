# Supporting Legacy Content Review

Review date: 2026-10-07  
Status: Phase 1 inventory decision; not public website copy

## Purpose

This review evaluates supporting material from the current DokuWiki and local
IVC collection for ideas worth preserving in the new website. It does not
assume that the legacy page structure, wording, or artifacts should be migrated.

The review applies the foundation decisions in
[foundation-messaging-brief.md](foundation-messaging-brief.md) and Nathan's
scope decisions recorded below.

## Scope decisions confirmed by Nathan

### Code-system listings and quality metrics

Do not include a catalog of vaccine code systems, comparative scores, or
code-system quality pages in this version of the website.

The legacy metrics were generated using code developed by François. Alignment
tools, metrics, definitions, quality controls, versioning, and publication
processes are being redesigned. Static scores would quickly become stale and
could be mistaken for current IVC judgments or recommendations.

The website may explain at a high level that alignment work can expose gaps and
support future assessment. It must not reproduce legacy completeness,
precision, maintenance, licensing, informativeness, tolerance, inclusiveness,
or translatability scores. When the new system and governance are ready, the
website can link to their authoritative output.

### Language

The initial website will support English only. Do not migrate the Spanish
website section or Spanish-language outreach, role, agenda, and planning pages.

Spanish-language presentations that belong to historical meetings remain
eligible for preservation with those meetings in InteropHub. Their historical
language should not be changed. The local Spanish source collection remains an
internal archive; this is a website-scope decision, not authorization to delete
source files.

Future multilingual support should be designed deliberately rather than
recreating the old Spanish section page by page.

### Country and organization research

Do not migrate country profiles, organization profiles, interview plans, or
research summaries into the website. Existing material is incomplete and not
reliable enough to present as a maintained public resource.

InteropHub is the intended system for tracking this work if it resumes. The
website may eventually link to reviewed InteropHub records, but it should not
maintain a parallel research collection.

## Recommended FAQ content pattern

Do not migrate the old FAQ verbatim. A short new FAQ could answer questions that
remain useful after the foundational pages are written:

1. **Why do multiple vaccine code systems exist?**
   Different systems serve different jurisdictions and purposes. IVC supports
   understanding and alignment; it does not assume that one system should
   replace them all.
2. **Is IVC creating one universal vaccine code system?**
   No. IVC supports many code systems. NUVA is a shared ontology and alignment
   resource, not a mandate to replace local systems.
3. **Does IVC choose or certify code systems?**
   No. Owners and programs make their own decisions. IVC can provide evidence,
   alignment feedback, tools, and community perspectives.
4. **What is NUVA?**
   Use the technically reviewed answer from François's fact-check package.
5. **Can I bring IVC a technical question?**
   Yes, through the stated contact route or a monthly meeting. Support is
   peer-based and best effort; IVC is not an authority and cannot guarantee an
   answer or response time.
6. **Who participates in IVC?**
   Individuals who create, maintain, align, implement, or rely on immunization
   vocabularies. They share experience as individuals rather than formally
   representing their organizations or jurisdictions.
7. **How can I participate?**
   Attend a monthly meeting, bring a question, share relevant experience, or
   contribute to a clearly defined task. Do not recreate formal volunteer roles
   that have not been established.

These are content candidates, not a commitment to create a standalone FAQ page.
If the About, NUVA, Meetings, and Participate pages answer them directly, a
separate FAQ may be unnecessary.

## Glossary recommendation

Do not reproduce the legacy acronym catalog. Use this hierarchy instead:

1. Spell out and define a specialized term on first use.
2. Add short contextual definitions near technical content.
3. Create a small glossary only after page drafts reveal terms that recur and
   cannot be explained cleanly inline.
4. Include only vocabulary needed to understand IVC and NUVA; do not import a
   general AIRA, US immunization-program, or health-IT acronym list.
5. Link to authoritative external definitions when IVC does not own the term.

Likely glossary candidates, subject to François's review, include vaccine code
system, vocabulary, ontology, administered vaccine, abstract vaccine, valence,
alignment, semantic pivot, and lifetime immunization history. Acronyms such as
FHIR, HL7, IIS, EIR, SNOMED CT, and NUVA should be defined only where used.

## Participation recommendation

The new website should not recreate a volunteer program or jurisdiction-based
representation structure. A simple participation section should provide:

- Who the collaboration is primarily for
- The stable InteropHub meeting link
- What to expect from a monthly peer meeting
- How to bring a technical question
- How to offer experience or help on a defined piece of work
- The best-effort and non-authoritative nature of support
- A note that participation does not imply representation or endorsement by an
  employer, program, country, or jurisdiction

Before publication, confirm:

- End-to-end delivery to `info@ivci.org`
- That François still receives messages and Nathan receives the intended copy
- Who can update the mailbox or forwarding configuration if the test fails
- What privacy notice is needed for submitted contact information
- Whether meeting registration occurs exclusively through InteropHub
- Whether any response expectation should be stated beyond "best effort"

## One-page summary recommendation

The old overview should remain a historical artifact. A replacement one-page
summary may be valuable after the website's Home, About, and NUVA language is
approved. It should be generated from the same maintained source rather than
becoming an independent set of claims.

A future summary would need only:

- Why vaccine vocabularies matter
- Who IVC supports
- What IVC currently does
- What IVC does not do
- NUVA's relationship to the work
- How to attend a meeting or ask a question
- A review date and link to the website

## Material worth preserving as ideas

The legacy collection repeatedly identifies several durable ideas:

- Vaccine vocabulary work is essential infrastructure that often receives less
  attention than vaccine development, delivery, or policy.
- Code-set authors and maintainers frequently work behind the scenes and may
  lack peers with comparable immunization-specific expertise.
- Long-lived immunization histories must represent historical as well as
  current vaccinations.
- Multiple vocabularies exist for legitimate reasons; the challenge is
  preserving and comparing meaning, not eliminating every difference.
- Questions the collaboration cannot answer can still be clarified and routed
  more effectively.
- Peer evidence can help an expert explain a representational gap without IVC
  overruling the responsible organization.
- Meetings and lightweight technical tools can support practical learning even
  without a formal authority or funded service.

These ideas are already represented more accurately in the foundation brief.
They should not be quoted from legacy campaign or fundraising copy.

## Phase 1 conclusion for this collection

The supporting legacy material has been reviewed sufficiently for website
planning. It contributes useful problem statements, audience insights, and
visitor questions, but no supporting page should be migrated unchanged. The new
website should use the approved foundation, a small set of rewritten visitor
questions, simple participation guidance, and authoritative external links.
